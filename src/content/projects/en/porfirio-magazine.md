## The problem

Running a small online magazine means doing several jobs that have nothing to do with writing. Every article aimed at both an Italian and an English audience needs a translation, which costs either an external translator or an editor's afternoon. Promoting each piece on Instagram means designing carousel images by hand, one slide at a time, for every publication. Then there is everything a publication needs around the content: reader accounts, comments and their moderation, donations, and some visibility into what readers actually do. Off-the-shelf CMS platforms cover parts of this, but stitching together a CMS, a translation service, a social design tool, a payment processor and an analytics product leaves the editorial team managing five subscriptions and the gaps between them. For a magazine run by a small team, the cost of these chores is not abstract: it is the difference between publishing every week and publishing when there is time left over.

## What I built

Porfirio Magazine is the platform behind a real magazine, live at magazine.porfirioholding.com. It is a React single-page application talking to a Flask REST API with a PostgreSQL database, deployed on Render. Editors write, schedule and categorize articles in a rich-text editor; readers browse in Italian or English, comment, save favorites and donate through Stripe. Two editorial chores are automated. Articles are translated between Italian and English on demand by Google Gemini, and each translation is cached so the model is only called again if the article actually changes. The Instagram carousel for an article is generated by the backend: Gemini writes the slide copy within per-slide character limits, the slides are rendered as styled HTML cards, captured as images, and delivered as a ready-to-post ZIP. Around this sit accounts with Google login and OTP email verification, role-based permissions with a moderation queue, an analytics dashboard, and a Capacitor wrapper that packages the same frontend as an iOS app.

## How it works

The backend is a Flask application assembled by a factory, registering more than 30 route blueprints over roughly 22 SQLAlchemy models; the frontend counts about 178 React components across about 27 routed pages, with everything but the home page lazy-loaded for code splitting. The least obvious choice is the authentication layer: a request can carry either a Flask-Login session cookie or a JWT bearer token, both resolved in a single custom request_loader, so the same API serves the web SPA and token clients without duplicating endpoints. Translation caching is keyed on a SHA-256 hash of the article content, which makes invalidation automatic: edit the article and the hash changes. Instagram slides are screenshots of rendered HTML taken with headless Playwright, rather than images drawn programmatically, then uploaded to Cloudinary. Celery workers on a Redis broker handle email; Redis also backs rate limiting and caching. The render.yaml manifest describes three services plus the managed PostgreSQL database, with a weekly cron cleaning logs.

## Results

The magazine is in production: deployed on Render with custom domains and a managed PostgreSQL database, and actively maintained. Donations flow through Stripe, with a webhook endpoint on the server and feature flags to switch donations on or off per environment. Engagement is measured: per-article metrics and Instagram-generation statistics are recorded, charted in the dashboard with Recharts, and exportable to CSV.

## Limits and what I'd redo

Translation happens on demand, so the first reader who opens an article in the other language waits for the Gemini call; translating at publish time would move that cost off the reader's path. And the frontend carries four map and globe libraries (Google Maps, Mapbox GL, Leaflet, react-globe.gl) that grew one integration at a time; I would consolidate them into one, since each adds bundle weight the reader pays for.
